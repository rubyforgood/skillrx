Home page: our mission

The bottom of SkillRx's public home page, from the features band down. The recommended version leads with the mission as one plain statement, backs it with three facts that don't repeat the features above, shows subjects as labels rather than cards that look clickable, and drops an unchecked claim from the contact band. Wording is a draft for the SkillRx team.

1The mission is the headline

One plain sentence, large and dark, at reading width. Today it sits in the gray used for secondary text, beside a heading. "Our mission" becomes a small label above it.

The pattern on Khan Academy, Wikipedia and Doctors Without Borders' about pages.

2Three facts, not four slogans

Each says something a reader can check: it's free, providers publish it, it's in local languages. None repeats the features above, and they use the same icon tiles so the two bands look related.

Replaces "Always free and open", "Cutting-edge literature", "Worldwide accessibility", "Fits local needs".

3Subjects are labels, not cards

Bordered cards look clickable, and these do nothing. Pills read as labels. Full names: "Emergency medicine", "Infectious diseases". Topics need a sign-in, so they can't link for a visitor.

The list is typed into the page; the SkillRx team should confirm it.

4Drop the unchecked claim

"Thousands of healthcare professionals" isn't a number anyone has checked. Keep the offer of help, or replace the claim with a real figure.

Same reason "Get started today" was removed: nobody can sign up.
